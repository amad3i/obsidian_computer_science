# Cramér's decomposition theorem

Cramér's  decomposition theorem is a result in probability theory. It is well known that if random variables 
  
    
      
        
          ξ
          
            1
          
        
      
    
    
  
 and 
  
    
      
        
          ξ
          
            2
          
        
      
    
    
  
 are independent and normally distributed, then their sum is also normally distributed. It turns out that the converse statement is also true. This result was anticipated by Paul Lévy and proved by Harald Cramér. As a consequence, a new direction in probability theory emerged—the theory of decompositions of random variables into independent summands (the arithmetic of probability distributions).
