# Chang's conjecture

In model theory, a branch of mathematical logic, Chang's conjecture, attributed to Chen Chung Chang by Vaught (1963, p. 309), states that every model of type (ω2,ω1) for a countable language has an elementary submodel of type (ω1,ω). The axiom of constructibility implies that Chang's conjecture fails. Silver proved the consistency of Chang's conjecture from the consistency of an ω1-Erdős cardinal. Hans-Dieter Donder showed a weak version of the reverse implication: if Chang's conjecture is not only consistent but actually holds, then ω2 is ω1-Erdős in K.
More generally, Chang's conjecture for two pairs (α,β), (γ,δ) of cardinals is the claim
that every model of type (α,β) for a countable language has an elementary submodel of type (γ,δ).
