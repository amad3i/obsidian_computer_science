# Hopcroft's problem

In computational geometry, Hopcroft's problem is the problem of testing, for a given system of points and lines in the Euclidean plane, whether at least one of the points lies on at least one of the lines. More generally, one may ask for the number of point–line incidences. Both versions of the problem can be solved in time

Its computational complexity is closely connected to the complexity of several other problems in computational geometry, including that of three-dimensional Euclidean minimum spanning trees.
